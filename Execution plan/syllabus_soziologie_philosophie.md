# Soziologie & Philosophie — Counterfactual BA Syllabi

**What this is.** The disciplinary formation you would have received had you read *Soziologie* or *Philosophie* instead of economics — built as a German university BA runs (theory spine + methods + subfields for sociology; historical sequence + systematic subfields for philosophy), with the Anglo-American canon a well-rounded student now meets regardless folded in. It is a *map of formation*, not a schedule. Overlap with the core Sozialphilosophie programme is deliberate, not avoided.

**How each author is listed.**
- **Concepts —** the key terms you would carry away.
- **Read —** the texts worth the time (entry points flagged where the whole is too much).
- **Why —** why the author is formative, for the discipline and for your endpoint specifically.
- **On the plan —** whether the core programme already covers this **as assigned reading**, and where. *Yes* (assigned) / *Partial* (some of it assigned) / *Marginal* (named or discussed as an opponent/analogy, but not itself read) / *No*. **The trap this file initially fell into:** an author *discussed* in a module (e.g. Popper in the Positivismusstreit) is **not** thereby "on the plan" as reading — that distinction is now enforced.

**Priority marks.** **[+]** = genuinely additive and high-ROI for the Sozialphilosophie endpoint; unmarked = standard formation, worth knowing but peripheral to your goal.

**Accuracy caveat.** Characterisations of authors, concepts, and intellectual lineages are reliable at this level. Bibliographic specifics (editions, exact subtitles) and the "On the plan" tags are high-confidence-but-spot-checked, not exhaustively verified against every appendix — treat them as a working map. The main residual risk in the "On the plan" tags is the *discussed-vs-assigned* distinction now flagged above: a "Yes"/"Partial" that actually rests on an author being *referenced or argued against* rather than *read* (the philosophy-of-science entry below was one such error, corrected 2026-07-29). A full verification pass against the module reading-lists is available on request. Where I assert a lineage (Fichte→Honneth on recognition, Husserl→Habermas on lifeworld, Austin→Habermas on speech acts), it is standard and well-attested, but pin the passage before citing in writing.

# Part I — Soziologie (BA)

The shape of the degree: a **Theorie** spine (Klassiker → Gegenwart), a large **Methoden** sequence (quantitative + qualitative), and a sampling of **spezielle Soziologien**. Your programme is unusually strong on the theory spine, thin on the classical middle, and absent on qualitative methods.

## A. Klassische Theorie

### Karl Marx (1818–1883)
- **Concepts —** class, *Entfremdung* (alienation), ideology, base/superstructure, *Verdinglichung* (reification, via Lukács), mode of production.
- **Read —** *Ökonomisch-philosophische Manuskripte* (1844, alienation); *Die deutsche Ideologie* Part I (ideology, historical materialism); *Das Kapital* Bd. I ch. 1 (commodity fetishism) & ch. 24 (so-called primitive accumulation).
- **Why —** Read here *as sociologist* — the theorist of class formation, ideology, and the social constitution of economic categories — a different register from the *Kritik der politischen Ökonomie* (critique of political economy) your programme runs. Sociology's entire conflict tradition (Dahrendorf, Bourdieu, critical theory) descends from this reading.
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 2 (but as critique of political economy; the sociological Marx of stratification is lighter).

### Max Weber (1864–1920)
- **Concepts —** *Verstehen* (interpretive understanding), *Idealtypus* (ideal type), *Werturteilsfreiheit* (value-freedom), *Rationalisierung* / *Entzauberung* (rationalisation / disenchantment), *Herrschaft* (legitimate domination: traditional / charismatic / legal-rational), bureaucracy, class vs. *Stand* (status) vs. party, the "iron cage" (*stahlhartes Gehäuse*).
- **Read —** *Die protestantische Ethik und der Geist des Kapitalismus*; *Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft* (selections: types of action §§1–2; *Herrschaftssoziologie*); the *Wissenschaftslehre* essays (*"Objektivität"*, *"Wissenschaft als Beruf"*).
- **Why —** The other founding pole to Marx, and the deepest single source for your endpoint: Habermas's rationalisation thesis in TKH (*Theorie des kommunikativen Handelns*) is a critical appropriation of Weber, and the *Werturteilsfreiheit* question is exactly what the Positivismusstreit contests. Interpretive method (*Verstehen*) and the ideal type are the methodological vocabulary the whole tradition presupposes.
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 3.

### Émile Durkheim (1858–1917) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** *fait social* (social fact) as *sui generis*, mechanical vs. organic solidarity, *anomie*, the non-contractual elements of contract, collective effervescence, collective representations.
- **Read —** *Die Regeln der soziologischen Methode* (social facts); *Über die Teilung der sozialen Arbeit* (solidarity, anomie); *Der Selbstmord* (the founding quantitative study); *Die elementaren Formen des religiösen Lebens* (religion, the social origin of the categories).
- **Why —** The discipline's paradigm of what "social explanation" *is* — *Suicide* shows social facts explaining rates no individual-level story reaches. *Anomie* is the direct ancestor of the "social pathology" vocabulary Honneth and Jaeggi use, and Durkheim's social integration feeds (via Parsons) Habermas's *System*/*Lebenswelt* distinction. The non-contractual-elements-of-contract argument is a sharp economist's bridge.
- **On the plan —** Partial: Appendix E has *Teilung der Arbeit* Bk III only; the *Regeln*, *Suicide*, and *Elementary Forms* are new — and they are the parts a sociologist actually carries.

### Georg Simmel (1858–1918) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** *Wechselwirkung* (reciprocal effect), formal sociology, social differentiation, the *Fremde* (the stranger), the *Großstadt* (metropolis) and mental life, the tragedy of culture, sociability, conflict as a form of sociation.
- **Read —** *Philosophie des Geldes*; the essays *"Exkurs über den Fremden"* and *"Die Großstädte und das Geistesleben"*; *Soziologie* (1908, selections).
- **Why —** The underrated one for you: *Philosophie des Geldes* is a cultural-theoretic account of monetisation that speaks straight to the economist and is the ancestor of reification-talk (it shaped Lukács and Benjamin). Simmel's formal sociology — studying the *forms* of association independent of content — is a distinct methodological option the discipline keeps returning to.
- **On the plan —** Partial: Appendix E has the final chapter of *Philosophie des Geldes*; the rest is new.

### Ferdinand Tönnies (1855–1936) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** *Gemeinschaft* (community) vs. *Gesellschaft* (association), *Wesenwille* vs. *Kürwille*.
- **Read —** *Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft* (1887, Introduction + Bk I).
- **Why —** A German-specific staple that sits structurally behind a century of *Zeitdiagnose* (diagnosis of the times) — the community/association distinction is the template every "loss of community" narrative runs, from Weber through Habermas's colonisation thesis to Putnam.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Comte / Spencer / Pareto & Mosca — orientation founders
- **Concepts —** positivism and the "law of three stages" (Comte, who coined *sociologie*); social evolution and functional differentiation (Spencer); the circulation of elites (Pareto, Mosca).
- **Read —** orientation level only — a good history-of-social-theory chapter (Joas & Knöbl, *Sozialtheorie*, covers them).
- **Why —** The prehistory that Weber and Durkheim define themselves against; know the names and the positions, not the primary texts.
- **On the plan —** No (orientation only).

### George Herbert Mead (1863–1931)
- **Concepts —** the social self, the "I" and the "me", taking the role of the other, the *generalised other*, symbolic interaction, gesture and significant symbol.
- **Read —** *Geist, Identität und Gesellschaft* (*Mind, Self and Society*, 1934).
- **Why —** The social-psychological engine of your endpoint: Honneth's *Kampf um Anerkennung* (struggle for recognition) reconstructs Mead directly, and the intersubjective self is Habermas's route past the philosophy of consciousness. Symbolic interactionism (Blumer, Goffman) is his sociological progeny.
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 4A (Pragmatism).

## B. Die mittlere Generation

### Talcott Parsons (1902–1979) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** structural functionalism, the social system, AGIL, pattern variables, the problem of double contingency, social order.
- **Read —** *The Structure of Social Action* (1937, selections); orientation via Joas & Knöbl rather than the full *Social System*.
- **Why —** The theory your programme uses without naming: Habermas's *System*/*Lebenswelt* apparatus in TKH Vol. 2 and Luhmann's whole systems theory are critical rewrites of Parsons. He is also the man who translated Weber and Durkheim into a single American theory. A focused orientation pays for itself when you reach TKH.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (referenced through Habermas, Module 6; no dedicated treatment).

### Robert K. Merton (1910–2003) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** middle-range theory, manifest and latent functions, the self-fulfilling prophecy, reference groups, the strain/anomie theory of deviance, the ethos of science (CUDOS).
- **Read —** *Social Theory and Social Structure* (1949, key essays: "Manifest and Latent Functions", "Social Structure and Anomie").
- **Why —** The model of theory that *touches data* — the corrective to a theory-heavy formation. "Latent function" is a workhorse concept for any structural analysis, and his anomie-as-strain reworking of Durkheim is a BA cornerstone. Cheap, high formative value.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Norbert Elias (1897–1990) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** figurational sociology, the *Zivilisationsprozess* (civilising process), the entwining of psychogenesis and sociogenesis, *Habitus* (before Bourdieu), the established and the outsiders, *Fremdzwang* → *Selbstzwang* (external → self-constraint).
- **Read —** *Über den Prozeß der Zivilisation* (1939, Vol. I + the theoretical synthesis in Vol. II).
- **Why —** The great German processual-historical alternative to static structural theory, and directly relevant to your *Bildung* / socialisation interests: it explains how affect-regulation and self-control are built by long-run social interdependence. His *Habitus* concept feeds Bourdieu.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Alfred Schütz (1899–1959) & Berger / Luckmann
- **Concepts —** the *Lebenswelt* (lifeworld) as sociological object, typifications, the stock of knowledge, intersubjectivity, the social construction of reality, institutionalisation, plausibility structures.
- **Read —** Schütz, *Der sinnhafte Aufbau der sozialen Welt* (1932, selections); Berger & Luckmann, *Die gesellschaftliche Konstruktion der Wirklichkeit* (1966).
- **Why —** The hinge from Husserl's phenomenology to sociology — and the point where the *Lebenswelt* concept enters social theory on its way to Habermas. Berger & Luckmann is the most-assigned single title in the discipline; it is where "social construction" comes from, before the phrase went slack.
- **On the plan —** Partial (Schütz and Berger/Luckmann referenced via the social-epistemology strand; no dedicated module).

### Erving Goffman (1922–1982) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** dramaturgy, front/back stage, impression management, the interaction order, *frame* analysis, *stigma*, total institutions, face-work.
- **Read —** *Wir alle spielen Theater* (*The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*); *Stigma*; *Asylums*.
- **Why —** The micro-sociologist of the self in interaction. *Stigma* is, in effect, an empirical phenomenology of misrecognition — the natural sociological partner to Honneth's *Missachtung* (disrespect/misrecognition). Reading Goffman gives the recognition vocabulary an observational body.
- **On the plan —** No (one passing mention).

### Garfinkel / Blumer / the Chicago School
- **Concepts —** ethnomethodology and *breaching experiments*, the accountability of action, indexicality (Garfinkel); symbolic interactionism as method (Blumer); urban ecology and fieldwork (Park & Burgess).
- **Read —** Garfinkel, *Studies in Ethnomethodology* (1967, ch. 1–2); Blumer, *Symbolic Interactionism* (1969, ch. 1).
- **Why —** The radical-interpretive wing: social order as an ongoing practical accomplishment of members, not a structure imposed on them. The Chicago School is the origin of ethnographic fieldwork as a discipline-defining craft.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Karl Mannheim (1893–1947)
- **Concepts —** *Wissenssoziologie* (sociology of knowledge), the existential situatedness of thought (*Seinsverbundenheit*), ideology vs. utopia, relationism, the free-floating intelligentsia.
- **Read —** *Ideologie und Utopie* (1929).
- **Why —** Systematises the ideology problem into a research programme and asks the reflexive question critical theory must answer: if all thought is socially situated, what secures the critic's own standpoint? Feeds Bohnsack's documentary method in contemporary German methodology.
- **On the plan —** No (marginal).

### Exchange theory — Homans (1910–1989) & Blau (1918–2002)
- **Concepts —** social behaviour as exchange, reciprocity, distributive justice, the emergence of power from imbalanced exchange.
- **Read —** Blau, *Exchange and Power in Social Life* (1964, selections).
- **Why —** The current in sociology closest to your rational-choice instincts — useful *precisely* as the foil the Frankfurt tradition rejects: it shows what a systematically economistic sociology looks like, so you can locate where and why critical theory refuses it.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Ralf Dahrendorf (1929–2009)
- **Concepts —** conflict theory, *Homo Sociologicus* and role theory, authority and class in post-Marxist industrial society, life chances.
- **Read —** *Soziale Klassen und Klassenkonflikt* (1957); *Homo Sociologicus* (1958).
- **Why —** The German liberal reconstruction of class conflict without Marx's economics — and a sharp critic of role theory's coercive side. A staple of the German BA that an Anglo list omits.
- **On the plan —** No.

## C. Zeitgenössische Theorie

### Jürgen Habermas (1929–)
- **Concepts —** *kommunikatives Handeln* (communicative action), *System* vs. *Lebenswelt*, the *Kolonialisierung der Lebenswelt* (colonisation of the lifeworld), *Diskursethik* (discourse ethics), the public sphere (*Öffentlichkeit*), the ideal speech situation.
- **Read —** on plan (see programme).
- **Why —** Your programme's centre of gravity; listed here to place him in the sociological lineage (Weber + Durkheim + Mead + Parsons synthesised).
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 6.

### Niklas Luhmann (1927–1998)
- **Concepts —** *autopoiesis*, functional differentiation, *doppelte Kontingenz* (double contingency), communication (not action) as the elementary social operation, system/environment, *Sinn* (meaning), second-order observation.
- **Read —** on plan; *Soziale Systeme* (1984) is the anchor.
- **Why —** The sharpest antagonist to normative critical theory — he brackets normativity entirely and out-describes Habermas at the level of social complexity. The Habermas–Luhmann debate (*Theorie der Gesellschaft oder Sozialtechnologie?*, 1971) is a foundational sparring match.
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 11B.

### Pierre Bourdieu (1930–2002)
- **Concepts —** *Habitus*, *Feld* (field), the forms of capital (economic / cultural / social / symbolic), symbolic violence, *Distinktion*, misrecognition (*méconnaissance*), reproduction, the *illusio*.
- **Read —** on plan; *Die feinen Unterschiede* (*La distinction*) is the anchor.
- **Why —** The leading modern theorist of how domination reproduces through culture and everyday practice — and a live interlocutor for the recognition/redistribution debate on your plan.
- **On the plan —** Yes, Module 11C.

### Michel Foucault (1926–1984)
- **Concepts —** power/knowledge, *Disziplinarmacht* (disciplinary power), the panopticon, *Biopolitik*, governmentality, genealogy, the *dispositif*, subjectivation.
- **Read —** on plan; *Überwachen und Strafen* (*Surveiller et punir*) is the anchor.
- **Why —** The great alternative theory of power to the Frankfurt tradition; the Habermas–Foucault confrontation (Fraser; the modernity lectures in *Der philosophische Diskurs der Moderne*) and Honneth's *Kritik der Macht* are core to your critical self-location.
- **On the plan —** Partial (Module 11A / *Surveiller et punir* selections; the biopolitics/governmentality arc is lighter).

### Anthony Giddens (1938–)
- **Concepts —** *Strukturation* (the duality of structure), the reflexive project of the self, ontological security, disembedding, high/late modernity.
- **Read —** *Die Konstitution der Gesellschaft* (*The Constitution of Society*, 1984); *Modernity and Self-Identity* (1991).
- **Why —** The most influential attempt to dissolve the structure/agency dualism — structure as both medium and outcome of practice. A standard reference point for how modernity reshapes selfhood.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (Appendix E orientation).

### Ulrich Beck (1944–2015) / Zygmunt Bauman (1925–2017) / Hartmut Rosa (1965–)
- **Concepts —** *Risikogesellschaft* and reflexive modernisation, individualisation (Beck); liquid modernity, the ambivalence of modernity (Bauman); social acceleration and *Resonanz* as the criterion of the good life (Rosa).
- **Read —** Beck, *Risikogesellschaft* (1986); Bauman, *Liquid Modernity* (2000); Rosa, *Beschleunigung* (2005) & *Resonanz* (2016).
- **Why —** The contemporary *Zeitdiagnose* canon — the genre your Phase III writing may itself enter. Rosa is a Frankfurt (Jena) heir who reconnects diagnosis to a positive normative criterion.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Appendix E; Rosa also Module 8).

### Latour / Collins / Alexander — frontier options
- **Concepts —** actor-network theory and the symmetry of humans and non-humans (Latour); interaction ritual chains and emotional energy (Collins); the "strong program" in cultural sociology and social performance (Alexander).
- **Read —** Latour, *Eine neue Soziologie für eine neue Gesellschaft* (*Reassembling the Social*, 2005), Introduction.
- **Why —** The live contemporary challenges to the very idea of "social explanation": Latour denies that "the social" explains anything; Alexander re-centres meaning and culture against structural reduction. Breadth for knowing where the discipline's frontier arguments now run.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (Latour in Appendix E orientation; Collins, Alexander not).

### Gender & critical-diagnostic — Fraser / Connell / Smith
- **Concepts —** the redistribution/recognition dilemma, participatory parity (Fraser); hegemonic masculinity, the gender order (Connell); standpoint theory, the relations of ruling (Smith).
- **Read —** Fraser (on plan); Connell, *Masculinities* (1995); Smith, *The Everyday World as Problematic* (1987).
- **Why —** Fraser is already central to your plan; Connell and Smith give the gender-theoretic and standpoint-epistemology context her intervention answers.
- **On the plan —** Fraser: Yes, Module 7. Connell / Smith: No.

## D. Methoden — your real gap **[+]**

**Quantitative methods — skip.** Survey design, sampling, regression, causal inference sit well below your level; this is your comparative advantage in the whole programme, not a gap.

**Philosophy-of-method layer (you partly have this):** the *Erklären/Verstehen* controversy (explanation vs. understanding) — von Wright, *Explanation and Understanding* (1971); Apel, *Die Erklären-Verstehen-Kontroverse* (1979); Gadamer on the hermeneutic side. *Your Greshoff et al.,* Verstehen und Erklären, *sits here — it stages the meta-debate; it does not teach interpretive craft.*
- **On the plan —** Yes (Module 5, Positivismusstreit + Hermeneutics).

**Interpretive-craft layer (the genuine hole).** None of the three below is what normative reconstruction *is* — that is philosophical hermeneutics applied to institutions (Gadamer, Hegel's own method). What they give is the sociological wing of the same stance: getting from a transcript to a warranted claim about latent meaning. They form a spectrum.

### Grounded Theory (Glaser & Strauss, 1967)
- **Concepts —** theory *generation* from data, *konstante Vergleich* (constant comparison), open → axial → selective coding, theoretical sampling, *theoretische Sättigung* (saturation), memoing; epistemologically driven by **Peircean abduction**.
- **Read —** Jörg Strübing, *Grounded Theory* (VS Verlag) — the German authority, reads GT through Mead/Dewey/Peirce; source texts: Glaser & Strauss, *The Discovery of Grounded Theory* (1967); the Glaser–Strauss/Corbin schism (Strauss & Corbin 1990 vs. Glaser, *Emergence vs. Forcing*, 1992); Charmaz, *Constructing Grounded Theory* (2006, constructivist third wave).
- **Why —** *Middle of the spectrum.* As procedure, an empirical tool you won't use; as epistemology — abductive, pragmatist, theory-from-the-case — genuinely relevant and tied straight to your Pragmatism module.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Qualitative Inhaltsanalyse (Mayring)
- **Concepts —** rule-guided, reliability-checkable category analysis; *Zusammenfassung* / *Explikation* / *Strukturierung*; *induktive* vs. *deduktive Kategorienbildung*.
- **Read —** Philipp Mayring, *Qualitative Inhaltsanalyse* (Beltz, ~13th ed.) — skim; Udo Kuckartz, *Qualitative Inhaltsanalyse* (Beltz Juventa) as the software-oriented alternative.
- **Why —** *The procedural pole — and the trap.* The most comfortable for you (disciplined content analysis with inter-coder reliability) and therefore the *least* like normative reconstruction: it categorises manifest content and never reaches latent meaning. Know it exists and why it dominates German thesis work; don't spend a book on it.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Objektive Hermeneutik (Oevermann)
- **Concepts —** *latente Sinnstrukturen* (latent structures of meaning, objective and often against the actors' self-understanding), *Sequenzanalyse* (sequence analysis), *gedankenexperimentelle Lesarten* (thought-experimental readings), *Fallstruktur* / *Fallstrukturgesetzlichkeit* (case structure), interpretation as *Kunstlehre* (an art, not an algorithm).
- **Read —** **Andreas Wernet, *Einführung in die Interpretationstechnik der Objektiven Hermeneutik*** (VS Verlag) — the accessible entry that actually teaches sequence analysis; programmatic source: Oevermann et al. (1979) in Soeffner (Hrsg.), *Interpretative Verfahren* (hard — read *about* it first).
- **Why —** *The reconstructive pole — and the one that illuminates your programme.* Its constitutive move — reconstruct an objective latent structure *against* the actors' manifest self-understanding — is structurally the move critical theory makes in *immanente Kritik* and ideology-critique. Caveat: Oevermann's lineage is structuralist–Freudian–Parsonian–Peircean, not Frankfurt School; the resonance is an analogy of method, not shared ancestry.
- **On the plan —** No.

**Ranked reading (for understanding, not doing — one is enough):**
1. **Wernet, *Objektive Hermeneutik*** — short, teaches the craft, mirrors *immanente Kritik*. *If you read only one, this.*
2. **Jo Reichertz, *Die Abduktion in der qualitativen Sozialforschung*** (Springer VS) — the *logic* of interpretive inference (why it's abductive, not your econometrics); the one book that adds a *distinct* large insight. *Read this only if #1 catches fire.*
3. **Ralf Bohnsack, *Rekonstruktive Sozialforschung*** or **Jörg Strübing, *Qualitative Sozialforschung*** — the map of the whole reconstructive field; reference, not read-through.
- *Skip* Uwe Flick, *Qualitative Sozialforschung* (comprehensive catalogue, low insight-density) unless you want a reference shelf. **One area where German-language method is the frontier, not a compromise — stay German here.**

## E. Spezielle Soziologien

Standard breadth; sample ~3–4 by curiosity, mostly peripheral to your endpoint: social stratification & mobility; family & life course; *Bildungssoziologie* (Bourdieu/Passeron on reproduction); urban sociology; sociology of religion; political sociology & social movements; deviance/criminology (labelling; anomie/strain); *Wissenssoziologie* / STS; *Kultursoziologie*; migration & demography; gender.

### Wirtschaftssoziologie (economic sociology) — **[+]** the high-value special sociology for you
- **Concepts —** *embeddedness* and the strength of weak ties (Granovetter); the double movement and fictitious commodities (Polanyi); the social meaning of money (Zelizer); markets as fields / the architecture of markets (Fligstein, White).
- **Read —** Granovetter, "Economic Action and Social Structure: The Problem of Embeddedness" (1985); Polanyi, *The Great Transformation* (1944); Zelizer, *The Social Meaning of Money* (1994); Streeck (on plan).
- **Why —** Your own discipline turned inside-out — the sociological critique of the market as a socially constituted, not natural, order. It feeds directly into Honneth's *Der arbeitende Souverän* (the working sovereign) and the socialisation-of-the-economy debates, and it is where your training gives you an edge no philosopher has.
- **On the plan —** Polanyi: Yes (Streeck block / capitalism-critique modules). Granovetter, Zelizer, Fligstein/White: No. Streeck: Yes.

# Part II — Philosophie (BA)

Two interlocking structures: a **historische Sequenz** (ancient → 20th c.) and the **systematische Disziplinen** (the problem-areas). A German BA weights history and the continental strand; an Anglo BA weights the systematic subfields and analytic authors. Your programme is a deep vertical slice (Kant → Hegel → Marx → Kritische Theorie) through a much wider map.

## A. Historische Sequenz

### Plato (427–347 BCE) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** the Forms (*eidē*), the tripartite soul, justice as harmony, the philosopher-king, the divided line and the cave, *anamnesis*, dialectic.
- **Read —** *Politeia* (*Republic*) — the core; then *Symposion*, *Phaidon*, *Theaitetos* (knowledge), *Gorgias*.
- **Why —** The tap-root of all political philosophy and of the theory/appearance distinction everything downstream (including your Hegel) presupposes. The *Republic*'s question — what is justice, and what social order realises it — is the question your entire endpoint inherits.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (Appendix I lists *Parmenides* alone; the *Republic* is new).

### Aristotle (384–322 BCE)
- **Concepts —** *phronesis* (practical wisdom), *eudaimonia* as activity, virtue as a mean, *praxis* vs. *poiesis*, the *zōon politikon*, *energeia*/*dynamis* (actuality/potentiality), substance, the four causes.
- **Read —** *Nikomachische Ethik* Bks I & VI; *Politik* Bks I & III; *Metaphysik* Bk Θ (and Z–H for substance).
- **Why —** The single most load-bearing prerequisite your own docs already flag: "rational" in the Frankfurt tradition is Aristotelian practical reason, not decision theory; Hegel's *Sittlichkeit* and *Wirklichkeit* are Aristotle reworked; Honneth, Jaeggi, and MacIntyre all draw on the *NE*. ~12 hours here is the cheapest insurance against the economist's-framing trap.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Appendix I: NE I/VI, Met. Θ/Z–H elevated for this reader).

### Hellenistic philosophy — Stoa / Epikur / Skepsis
- **Concepts —** *apatheia* and living according to nature (Stoics); *ataraxia* and the swerve (Epicureans); suspension of judgement (*epoché*) (Skeptics).
- **Read —** Epiktet, *Handbüchlein*; Marcus Aurelius, *Selbstbetrachtungen*; Sextus Empiricus, *Grundriss der pyrrhonischen Skepsis* (selections).
- **Why —** The ancient art-of-living traditions Foucault returned to in his late work (*care of the self*), and the origin of *epoché*, which Husserl later transforms into phenomenological method.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Augustine (354–430) / medieval — Anselm, Aquinas, Ockham
- **Concepts —** the inner self and will, time and memory, the two cities (Augustine); the ontological argument (Anselm); natural law, the Five Ways, essence/existence (Aquinas); nominalism (Ockham).
- **Read —** Augustinus, *Confessiones* Bks X–XI; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* selections (natural law: I-II q.90–97).
- **Why —** Augustine invents the reflexive first-person interiority modern philosophy runs on; Aquinas's natural law is the distant ancestor of the justification tradition (Forst). Low priority for your endpoint, but part of a well-rounded formation and the medieval semester every German BA includes.
- **On the plan —** No.

### René Descartes (1596–1650) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** methodic doubt, the *cogito*, mind–body dualism, clear and distinct ideas, the epistemological turn (foundations before knowledge).
- **Read —** *Meditationen über die Erste Philosophie* (1641) — complete; short.
- **Why —** The founding text of modern philosophy, and the subject-position the entire post-Hegelian and Frankfurt tradition is trying to escape: the isolated, disembedded knowing subject. You cannot see what "intersubjectivity" and "the philosophy of consciousness" mean as critical targets without having met the Cartesian subject first.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** substance monism (*Deus sive natura*), the parallelism of thought and extension, the affects, *conatus*, adequate vs. inadequate ideas, freedom as understood necessity.
- **Read —** *Ethik* (1677) — Parts I, III, IV (selectively; the geometric method is dense).
- **Why —** A live afterlife in critical theory and beyond: Adorno's non-identity, the affect tradition, Negri's political Spinozism. The immanent, non-transcendent conception of nature and freedom is a resource the tradition keeps mining against Kantian dualism.
- **On the plan —** No (passing mentions only).

### Leibniz (1646–1716) / Berkeley (1685–1753)
- **Concepts —** monads, pre-established harmony, sufficient reason, the best of all possible worlds (Leibniz); immaterialism, *esse est percipi* (Berkeley).
- **Read —** Leibniz, *Monadologie*; Berkeley, *Prinzipien der menschlichen Erkenntnis* (selections).
- **Why —** The rationalist and idealist-empiricist extremes that frame the Kantian synthesis; sufficient reason is a principle Hegel and Heidegger both interrogate. Orientation level.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679)
- **Concepts —** the state of nature, the war of all against all, the social contract, sovereignty, authorisation, the mechanical account of man.
- **Read —** *Leviathan* (1651) — Parts I–II.
- **Why —** The founding move of modern political philosophy: legitimate order derived from the consent of self-interested individuals. The contractarian baseline Rousseau, Kant, Rawls, and Habermas all rework — and the purest statement of the strategic-rationality picture critical theory rejects as a *model of the social bond*.
- **On the plan —** No.

### John Locke (1632–1704)
- **Concepts —** empiricism and the *tabula rasa*, primary/secondary qualities, personal identity as psychological continuity, natural rights, property from labour, consent and the right of resistance.
- **Read —** *Versuch über den menschlichen Verstand* (selections: Bk II on ideas, Bk II.27 on identity); *Zwei Abhandlungen über die Regierung*, Second Treatise.
- **Why —** The founder of the empiricist tradition *and* of liberal political theory; the labour theory of property is a direct interlocutor for Marx and for any theory of just distribution. Foundational, low-difficulty.
- **On the plan —** No.

### David Hume (1711–1776) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** the problem of induction, causation as constant conjunction and habit, the bundle theory of the self, the **is/ought gap**, moral sentimentalism, the primacy of the passions.
- **Read —** *Eine Untersuchung über den menschlichen Verstand* (*Enquiry*); *Traktat* Bk III (morals) selections; *Dialoge über natürliche Religion*.
- **Why —** The is/ought problem is the hinge every meta-ethics and every justification-claim (Habermas's *Diskursethik*, Forst) has to clear; Kant's whole practical philosophy is the answer to Hume. The great naturalist and skeptic — knowing him sharpens exactly what the German tradition is resisting.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)
- **Concepts —** transcendental idealism, the categories, *Verstand* vs. *Vernunft*, the thing-in-itself, the antinomies, the categorical imperative, autonomy, the kingdom of ends, reflective judgement, the sublime.
- **Read —** on plan; the load-bearing selection (Appendix I) is *KrV* Aesthetic + Antinomies, *Grundlegung* complete, *Kritik der praktischen Vernunft* selectively, plus the political essays.
- **Why —** The non-negotiable prerequisite: your programme starts at Hegel, which *is* the response to Kant; discourse ethics is neo-Kantian, Forst's justification is Kantian. The single most load-bearing missing basic if it were missing — which is why your plan already builds it in.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Appendix I, load-bearing selection).

### Fichte (1762–1814) & Schelling (1775–1854)
- **Concepts —** the self-positing *Ich* and the origin of *Anerkennung* (recognition) in the summons/*Aufforderung* (Fichte); the Absolute as the point of indifference, *Naturphilosophie* (Schelling).
- **Read —** on plan (orientation, largely through Hegel's early polemics); Fichte, *Grundlage der gesamten Wissenschaftslehre* selections; *Grundlage des Naturrechts* (the recognition passages).
- **Why —** Fichte is the actual origin of the recognition concept Hegel systematises and Honneth revives — a direct upstream source for your endpoint that is easy to skip. Schelling is the road not taken that Heidegger and the later Frankfurt School (nature, non-identity) partly reopen.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Appendix I, orientation).

### G.W.F. Hegel (1770–1831)
- **Concepts —** dialectic and *Aufhebung*, *Geist*, *Sittlichkeit* (ethical life), *Anerkennung*, master/slave, determinate negation, *an sich / für sich*, the cunning of reason, the actuality of the rational.
- **Read —** on plan; *Phänomenologie des Geistes* (esp. ch. IV.A, VI) and *Grundlinien der Philosophie des Rechts*.
- **Why —** Your programme's foundation; listed to place him as the hinge of the whole sequence — the synthesis of Kant's autonomy with Aristotle's ethical life that everything from Marx to Honneth reworks.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Module 1 + Appendix I).

### Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** the world as will and representation, the primacy of the will, pessimism, aesthetic contemplation as suspension of willing, compassion-based ethics.
- **Read —** *Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung* Vol. I (selections: Bks I–IV).
- **Why —** The necessary background to Nietzsche, Freud, and the *Lebensphilosophie* the early Frankfurt School both draws on and reacts to; Horkheimer's late thought is markedly Schopenhauerian. The counter-Enlightenment reading of reason as instrument of the will prefigures *Dialektik der Aufklärung*.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** the aesthetic/ethical/religious stages, *Angst*, despair, the leap of faith, subjectivity as truth, the single individual against the crowd.
- **Read —** *Entweder – Oder* (selections); *Der Begriff Angst*; *Die Krankheit zum Tode*.
- **Why —** The founder of existential thought and a direct, small payoff for your plan: Adorno's *Habilitationsschrift* was *Kierkegaard: Konstruktion des Ästhetischen* (1933). The critique of Hegelian system from the standpoint of existence is a permanent counter-voice in the tradition.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900)
- **Concepts —** genealogy, master/slave morality and *ressentiment*, the will to power, perspectivism, the death of God and nihilism, eternal recurrence, the *Übermensch*.
- **Read —** on plan; *Zur Genealogie der Moral*, *Jenseits von Gut und Böse*, *Die fröhliche Wissenschaft*.
- **Why —** The deep background to *both* Foucault (genealogy as method) and *Dialektik der Aufklärung* (reason as domination); the most radical challenge to the presumption that critique stands on secure normative ground.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Phase I foundations / Module 4 references).

### John Stuart Mill (1806–1873)
- **Concepts —** utilitarianism (and the higher/lower pleasures revision), the harm principle, liberty of thought and expression, the tyranny of the majority.
- **Read —** *Der Utilitarismus*; *Über die Freiheit* (*On Liberty*).
- **Why —** The classic statement of both the utilitarian ethics and the liberalism that Rawls modifies and communitarians attack — the position the whole political-philosophy debate on your horizon defines itself against.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (referenced in the political-philosophy context).

### American Pragmatism — Peirce (1839–1914) / James (1842–1910) / Dewey (1859–1952)
- **Concepts —** the pragmatic maxim, abduction and fallibilism (Peirce); the will to believe, radical empiricism (James); inquiry, experience, democracy as a way of life, the reflex-arc critique (Dewey).
- **Read —** on plan; Dewey, *The Public and Its Problems*; Peirce, "The Fixation of Belief"; James, *Pragmatism* (selections).
- **Why —** Dewey's democratic theory feeds late Honneth ("Democracy as Reflexive Cooperation"); Peircean abduction is the logic of interpretive method (see the sociology methods section). The indigenous American counter-tradition to both positivism and idealism.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Module 4A; Dewey especially).

### Frege / Russell / early Wittgenstein — the analytic founding
- **Concepts —** sense and reference (*Sinn/Bedeutung*), the context principle (Frege); the theory of descriptions, logical atomism (Russell); the picture theory, "the limits of my language are the limits of my world", saying vs. showing (early Wittgenstein).
- **Read —** Frege, *"Über Sinn und Bedeutung"*; Russell, *"On Denoting"*; Wittgenstein, *Tractatus logico-philosophicus*.
- **Why —** The linguistic turn that founds analytic philosophy — the tradition against which "continental" is defined, and which your endpoint mostly brackets. Worth knowing where the fork happened.
- **On the plan —** No (marginal).

### Vienna Circle / logical positivism
- **Concepts —** the verification principle, the elimination of metaphysics, the unity of science, protocol sentences.
- **Read —** Carnap, *"Überwindung der Metaphysik durch logische Analyse der Sprache"*; Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic* (orientation).
- **Why —** Worth knowing what "positivism" actually claimed — and here is the twist my first pass obscured: the antagonist of the *Positivismusstreit* was **Popper's critical rationalism, not the Vienna Circle**; Popper was a sworn opponent of the verification principle. Knowing the real (and rather modest) Vienna Circle stops "positivism" from being a slur you can't cash out.
- **On the plan —** **No** (correcting my earlier "Yes as context"). "Positivism" is discussed as a target in Module 5, but neither the Vienna Circle's own texts nor Popper's are assigned — and the target is really Popper, not Carnap. Marginal at most.

### Later Wittgenstein (1889–1951)
- **Concepts —** meaning as use, *Sprachspiele* (language-games), *Lebensformen* (forms of life), rule-following and the private-language argument, family resemblance.
- **Read —** *Philosophische Untersuchungen* (esp. §§1–242).
- **Why —** *Lebensformen* is the term Jaeggi builds *Kritik von Lebensformen* on; the rule-following considerations underwrite the whole social-practice turn (Brandom's Hegel included). A direct source you use second-hand.
- **On the plan —** Partial (referenced via Jaeggi; not read directly).

### Austin / Searle / Grice **[+]**
- **Concepts —** speech acts, *locutionary/illocutionary/perlocutionary*, performatives, felicity conditions (Austin); the construction of social reality, direction of fit (Searle); conversational implicature, the cooperative principle (Grice).
- **Read —** Austin, *Zur Theorie der Sprechakte* (*How to Do Things with Words*); Searle, *Sprechakte* (selections); Grice, "Logic and Conversation".
- **Why —** Habermas's *Universalpragmatik* and the whole theory of communicative action are built directly on speech-act theory — this is load-bearing infrastructure you deploy without having read the source. The single cheapest way to see the machinery under TKH.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (used through Habermas; not read directly).

### Quine / Sellars / Kripke — mid-century analytic
- **Concepts —** the rejection of the analytic/synthetic distinction, the indeterminacy of translation, ontological relativity (Quine); the Myth of the Given, the space of reasons (Sellars); rigid designation, necessary a posteriori (Kripke).
- **Read —** Quine, *"Two Dogmas of Empiricism"*; Sellars, *"Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind"*; Kripke, *Name und Notwendigkeit* (selections).
- **Why —** Standard analytic formation; **Sellars** matters most for you — the attack on the Given and the "space of reasons" is the bridge McDowell and Brandom use to read Hegel, which touches your Module 1 secondary literature.
- **On the plan —** No (Sellars marginal via Brandom).

### Philosophy of science — Popper / Kuhn / Lakatos / Feyerabend / Hempel **[+]**
- **Concepts —** falsification and the demarcation problem (Popper); paradigms, normal vs. revolutionary science, incommensurability (Kuhn); research programmes (Lakatos); epistemological anarchism (Feyerabend); the covering-law model (Hempel).
- **Read —** Popper, *Logik der Forschung*; Kuhn, *Die Struktur wissenschaftlicher Revolutionen*; (deeper: Lakatos, "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes"; Feyerabend, *Wider den Methodenzwang*).
- **Why —** Popper's falsification and Kuhn's paradigms reshaped how every discipline — yours included — understands theory change; the natural economist's bridge into philosophy proper. And since Popper is the antagonist your Positivismusstreit module argues *against*, reading him first-hand repays that module directly rather than meeting him only through Adorno's polemic.
- **On the plan —** **No, as reading — this corrects my first-pass "Yes (Module 5)", which conflated "discussed as an opponent" with "assigned."** What the plan actually contains: the *Positivismusstreit* as a *debate* (Module 5 assigns Adorno's and Habermas's contributions; Popper is the named antagonist, but *Logik der Forschung* is not on the list); **Hempel's** covering-law model (Appendix B, item B.1.1, "the position everyone reacts against"); and the *Erklären/Verstehen* dispute (von Wright, Appendix B.1.5; Gadamer/Habermas, Module 5). **Kuhn** appears once, as a passing analogy (paradigm shifts vs. Bayesian updating); **Lakatos** and **Feyerabend** do not appear at all. So the philosophy-of-*natural*-science canon (falsification / paradigms / research programmes) is a genuine **[+]** gap; philosophy of *social* science and economics is, by contrast, well covered in Appendix B.

### John Rawls (1921–2002) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** justice as fairness, the original position and the veil of ignorance, the two principles and the difference principle, reflective equilibrium, the overlapping consensus, public reason, political liberalism.
- **Read —** *Eine Theorie der Gerechtigkeit* (1971) — read whole, not just about; *Politischer Liberalismus* (1993); the Habermas–Rawls exchange (*Journal of Philosophy*, 1995).
- **Why —** The most important 20th-c. work of political philosophy and the horizon Forst's justification project inhabits; Habermas debated him directly, Honneth positions against him. You currently have him only by reference — reading the *Theory* whole changes the resolution of everything downstream.
- **On the plan —** Partial (referenced; not read whole).

### Edmund Husserl (1859–1938)
- **Concepts —** intentionality, the phenomenological *epoché* and reduction, the *Lebenswelt* (lifeworld), the crisis of the European sciences, eidetic intuition.
- **Read —** *Die Krisis der europäischen Wissenschaften* (selections: the *Lebenswelt* sections); *Cartesianische Meditationen* (I–II).
- **Why —** The *Lebenswelt* concept enters social theory (via Schütz) and becomes Habermas's "lifeworld"; the *Krisis* critique of objectivism is an ancestor of the critique of instrumental reason. A source you stand on.
- **On the plan —** Partial (referenced; the lifeworld lineage assumed).

### Martin Heidegger (1889–1976) **[+]**
- **Concepts —** *Dasein*, being-in-the-world, *Zuhandenheit* (readiness-to-hand), *Geworfenheit* (thrownness), being-toward-death, authenticity/*Eigentlichkeit*, the ontological difference, later: *Gestell* (enframing), the question of technology.
- **Read —** *Sein und Zeit* (1927, §§1–44 core); *Die Frage nach der Technik* (essay).
- **Why —** The great German antipode: the target of Adorno's *Jargon der Eigentlichkeit*, the teacher of Gadamer and Marcuse, and the deepest 20th-c. challenge to the whole subject-based tradition. You cannot fully situate Adorno, Gadamer, or the critique of technology without him.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (referenced via Adorno's *Jargon*; not read directly).

### Sartre / Merleau-Ponty / de Beauvoir — existential phenomenology
- **Concepts —** existence precedes essence, radical freedom, bad faith, the look (Sartre); the primacy of embodied perception, the body-subject (Merleau-Ponty); the situation of woman, "one is not born a woman" (de Beauvoir).
- **Read —** Sartre, *Das Sein und das Nichts* (selections); Merleau-Ponty, *Phänomenologie der Wahrnehmung* (selections); de Beauvoir, *Das andere Geschlecht*.
- **Why —** The mid-century phenomenological development; de Beauvoir is foundational for feminist theory (and thus for Fraser/Butler downstream), Merleau-Ponty for embodiment and the critique of the disembodied Cartesian/Kantian subject.
- **On the plan —** No.

### Hans-Georg Gadamer (1900–2002)
- **Concepts —** philosophical hermeneutics, the fusion of horizons (*Horizontverschmelzung*), the rehabilitation of prejudice (*Vorurteil*) and tradition, effective history (*Wirkungsgeschichte*), the hermeneutic circle.
- **Read —** on plan; *Wahrheit und Methode* (Part II core).
- **Why —** The charter of philosophical hermeneutics and the interpretive stance your reconstruction practice presupposes; the Gadamer–Habermas debate (tradition vs. critique) is foundational to your Module 5 and to how critique relates to understanding.
- **On the plan —** Yes (Module 5).

### Structuralism & post-structuralism — Saussure / Lévi-Strauss / Derrida / Deleuze
- **Concepts —** the sign as arbitrary and differential, *langue*/*parole* (Saussure); structural anthropology, binary oppositions (Lévi-Strauss); *différance*, deconstruction, the metaphysics of presence (Derrida); difference and repetition, assemblages (Deleuze).
- **Read —** Saussure, *Grundfragen der allgemeinen Sprachwissenschaft* (selections); Derrida, *Die Schrift und die Differenz* (selections).
- **Why —** The French theoretical revolution that reframed subject, language, and structure; Foucault (on your plan) emerges from and against it. Breadth for reading the contemporary French interlocutors of critical theory.
- **On the plan —** Marginal (Foucault yes; the others no).

You will not do two more BAs on ~7–8 h/week already committed. Ranked by marginal value *for the Sozialphilosophie endpoint*, the things a well-rounded student has that you do not yet:
